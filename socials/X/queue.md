### Draft 49 (type: C beginner explainer, status: pending)
What's a "bear market rally"? It's a sharp jump in prices in the middle of a longer fall. Stocks drop a lot, bounce 10% or more, then drop again. The trouble is that in real time it looks exactly like a recovery. Only afterwards can you see which one it was. Knowing the term at least stops a good week looking like the end of the story.

### Draft 56 (type: C beginner explainer, status: pending)
What's an inverted yield curve? Normally a government pays you more interest to lend for 10 years than for 2, because waiting longer is riskier. Sometimes that flips and 2-year pays more. It happened before the 2001 and 2008 recessions, and again in 2022. It isn't a countdown timer though: the gap between the flip and the downturn has ranged from months to years.

### Draft 59 (type: E extras, myth vs reality, status: pending)
Myth: stocks always crash in October. Reality: 1929 and 1987 both happened in October, which is why the idea stuck. But on average, going back decades, September has been the weakest month for US stocks and October's record is mixed. Two famous crashes make a good story, not a pattern.
