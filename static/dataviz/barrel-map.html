📚 How traders play oil

Brent vs WTI. Brent (North Sea) is the global benchmark. WTI is the US benchmark and usually trades a little cheaper.

Futures. Most oil trading happens in futures contracts. When later months cost more than the front month that’s contango, often a sign of oversupply. The reverse (backwardation) signals tight supply.

What moves the price. OPEC+ output decisions, the weekly US inventory report (Wednesdays), global growth and demand, geopolitics, and the US dollar. Oil is priced in dollars, so a stronger dollar usually weighs on it.

Who feels it. Energy stocks and oil-exporting currencies like CAD and NOK tend to move with crude. Airlines and shipping costs move against it, and higher oil feeds into inflation.

Risk. Oil is volatile and leveraged products can lose money fast. This is education only, not financial advice.
