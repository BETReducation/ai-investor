Inventory tracker

How to read it. Under 5 days is tight, 5 to 15 is worth watching, above 15 is comfortable. LME stocks are only part of the picture: China and US warehouses matter too, and copper held in US warehouses is at record levels.

About this data
Prices and changes come from published market reports of 12–13 Nov 2026 (LME, SGX, Shanghai Metals Market, Trading Economics). Week moves are approximate and “n/a” means no verified figure. Record highs are the main published peaks and can differ by contract. Steel is China export hot-rolled coil, FOB; its record is approximate. Reserves are rounded USGS-style contained tonnes (bauxite and iron ore as ore; steel is annual output). Power prices and stockpile notes are approximate and qualitative. Power prices for countries without mining data are rough ballparks, since many have no single industrial tariff. Borders come from the open Datamaps world data. Exchange stocks are LME warehouse figures from mid-Nov 2026 and days of cover use rounded annual demand. Demand splits are rounded and approximate. Producer moves are shown only where verified. Supply shares are rounded approximations of recent USGS and IEA-style figures. Metal content of everyday items is typical and rounded. Exposure sensitivities and shock impacts are simple illustrations for learning, not forecasts or advice. The historic league uses rounded year-end benchmark prices for copper, aluminium, nickel, zinc and iron ore (iron ore from 2008); steel, lithium and cobalt lack a reliable long series, and 2026 is year to date. Educational and historical information only, not investment advice.
