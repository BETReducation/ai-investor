🫧 The Bubble Sky
What each clue means
  • P/E ratio: share price ÷ yearly profit per share. Under 20 is normal; over 35 means investors are paying for years of growth. Cisco’s was over 100 at the 2000 peak.
  • Price ÷ sales: price per unit of revenue. Under 5 is normal; over 15 means even big sales can’t justify the price.
  • Spend ÷ profit: money spent (investment or cash burn) for every unit of profit. Over 1× means spending more than it earns, usually funded by debt or faith.
  • Debt ÷ equity: borrowing compared with shareholders’ money. Over 2× is heavy, and bubbles are often built on borrowed money.
  • 12-month gain: over 80% in a year is parabolic, classic bubble behaviour.
  • Price vs 5-year average: over 3× its usual level is far from normal.

📜 Historic bubbles

How the score works

Each asset is scored 0–100 on five factors. Valuation comes from P/E (or the price vs its 5-year average where there is no P/E). Run-up combines the 12-month gain and the 5-year multiple. Fundamentals gap uses price ÷ sales and spend ÷ profit. Leverage uses debt ÷ equity. Sentiment is a hype estimate (for assets you add yourself it follows the run-up). Burst risk is a weighted average: valuation 30%, run-up 20%, fundamentals gap 25%, leverage 15%, sentiment 10%. Labels: under 50 Calm, 50–60 Warm, 60–70 Frothy, 70+ Bubble. The bubble stage (Calm waters to The bust) follows the classic bubble life-cycle. It is a teaching model, not a forecast. The 12-month gain, price vs 5-year average, P/E, price ÷ sales, spend ÷ profit (capital spending over net profit) and debt ÷ equity come live from Yahoo Finance where a match is found. Hype is always an estimate, and any asset without a live match keeps a rounded illustrative figure.
