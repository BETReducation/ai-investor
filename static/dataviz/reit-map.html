📚 REIT 101

What is a REIT? A company that owns income-producing property and pays most of its taxable income out as dividends (rules vary by country).

How do you make money? Dividends, plus any rise in the share price.

Main risks: interest rates, empty buildings and too much debt.

Two types: equity REITs own property; mortgage REITs lend against it.

UK corner

UK-REITs started in 2007. They must pay out at least 90% of their tax-exempt rental profits, and the tax treatment can differ from normal dividends, so check the rules. Well-known UK-listed REITs include Segro, Landsec, British Land, LondonMetric, Tritax Big Box, Derwent London and Unite Students. UK REIT shares can usually be held in an ISA or SIPP. This is education, not a recommendation.
