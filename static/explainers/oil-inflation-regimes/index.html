Oil Shocks and Inflation: 1973–74 and 2008

Oil surging while inflation climbs has happened before. Here is what US stocks, 10-year government bonds and gold actually did across the two best-known stretches, month by month, with oil and inflation underneath.

Educational and historical only. These charts show what happened in the past, not what will happen next, and nothing here is advice or a recommendation.

The numbers

Change from the first month to the last, and the deepest fall from a prior high inside the window. Returns include dividends (stocks) and coupons (bonds). Real figures are adjusted for CPI.

What the two stretches show

1973–74: inflation kept rising

Oil went from about $3.56 to $11.16 a barrel and CPI inflation climbed every few months, ending the window at 12.1%. Stocks fell 39% in nominal terms, or 50% once inflation is taken out. Ten-year bonds returned 7% nominal but lost 12% of their purchasing power. Gold rose by more than two and a half times in nominal terms and by about 133% in real terms.

2008–09: the spike reversed

Oil peaked at a $134 monthly average in June 2008 and was back near $39 by February 2009. Inflation hit 5.5% in July 2008 and turned negative by early 2009. Stocks fell as far as 43% below January 2008 (March 2009). Bonds rose as yields dropped, peaking 15% up at the end of 2008. Gold slipped about 8% by year-end, then recovered to 28% above its starting point by the end of 2009.

The oil headline was similar; what followed was not. In 1973–74 inflation became entrenched while growth weakened, which hurt both stocks and bonds in real terms. In 2008 the price spike gave way to a demand collapse and falling inflation, which favoured government bonds. Stocks lost between 43% and 50% of their real value at the low point in both cases. Two episodes are a small sample, and the starting conditions today differ from both.

Sources and how the series are built

Stocks: S&P composite from Robert Shiller's long-run dataset. Monthly total return is the change in the monthly average price plus one twelfth of the annual dividend, reinvested. Because Shiller uses monthly averages, the lows are slightly shallower than the worst daily closes.

Bonds: a constant-maturity 10-year Treasury, rebuilt from the FRED 10-year yield (GS10). Each month the bond bought at the prior yield is repriced at the new yield, plus a month of coupon. This is an approximation of a bond index, not an actual fund.

Gold: monthly gold price in US dollars (datasets/gold-prices, compiled from public gold-price histories). No income, so price change only.

Oil: FRED WTI spot price (WTISPLC), monthly. For 1973–74 this is largely a posted price that moved in steps, not a freely traded market price, which is why that line is stepped.

Inflation: US CPI-U (CPIAUCSL), year-on-year change. Real series divide each nominal value by the CPI level relative to the first month. The dotted line marks 4%.

Costs, taxes and currency effects are ignored. Window dates are fixed in advance (24 months from each January) rather than picked to flatter any asset. Rebuild the data with marketdata/build_regime_data.py.
