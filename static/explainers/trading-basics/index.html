Basic Trading: The Basics

Placing a trade comes down to four things: the type of order you use, what the trade can make or lose once costs are counted, where you decide to get out, and how big the position is. Each has a small tool to try, using made-up prices.

Educational and historical only. The prices are simulated and nothing here is advice or a recommendation.

The idea in one minute

A trade is a decision made in advance: why you are entering, how much you are risking, and where you will leave if it goes wrong or right. The mechanics are simple. Most of the skill is in the planning before the order goes in.

1. Order types

A market order trades now at the best price on offer. A limit order names your price and waits. A stop order stays dormant until the price hits a trigger. Each trades certainty of the price against certainty of being filled.

2. Profit, loss and costs

A long trade profits when the price rises, a short trade when it falls. Spread and commission are paid either way, so the price has to move past break-even before the trade earns anything.

3. Stops, targets and risk/reward

A stop-loss caps the loss and a take-profit banks the gain. The ratio of the two is the reward-to-risk ratio. A stop is not a guarantee: if the price jumps past it, the fill is worse.

4. Position size

Decide how much of the account you are willing to lose if the stop is hit, say 1%, then size the position to match. The distance to the stop sets the number of shares, not the other way round.
