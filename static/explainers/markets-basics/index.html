Understanding Markets: The Basics

A market is just a place, physical or online, where buyers and sellers agree prices. Four ideas explain most of what you see on a price chart: how supply and demand set the price, how buyers and sellers are matched, who is on the other side of your trade, and why prices move in cycles. Each has something to click.

Educational and historical only. The numbers are illustrative and nothing here is advice or a recommendation.

The idea in one minute

Prices are not set by anyone in charge. They emerge from thousands of people each deciding what they are willing to pay or accept. Understanding a market means understanding who those people are, how their orders meet, and what makes them all lean the same way at once.

1. Price comes from supply and demand

Buyers want low prices, sellers want high ones. The price settles where the quantity buyers want equals the quantity sellers offer. If more buyers arrive, the price tends to rise; if more sellers arrive, it tends to fall.

Watch for: the "supply" of a share is simply the holders willing to sell at a given price.

2. Bid, ask and spread

The bid is the highest price a buyer will pay. The ask is the lowest price a seller will accept. The gap is the spread, a small cost every trader pays. A market order takes the best price available now; a limit order names your price and waits.

Watch for: a big order can use up the best prices and fill at worse ones. That is slippage.

3. Many kinds of participant

Individuals, pension funds, hedge funds, market makers and algorithms all trade with different goals and time horizons. Some hold for decades, some for milliseconds. Prices reflect all of them at once.

Watch for: size matters. A few large participants can move a market that thousands of small ones cannot.

4. Prices move in cycles

Markets tend to rise for long stretches and then fall, driven partly by changing mood as well as by facts. A common description has four phases: accumulation, markup, distribution and markdown.

Watch for: the pattern is clear in hindsight and much harder to see in the moment.
